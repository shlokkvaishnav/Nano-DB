# Research decision log

Why things are the way they are, so the project doesn't depend on anyone's memory of it. Newest first. Add an entry whenever a research decision is made — a baseline chosen, a metric changed, a hypothesis rejected, a branch merged or archived — not just when something goes wrong.

**Tested this project's central prediction on the one system that could decide it, and it did not hold.**
PR #61 (`research/weaviate_dissociation/`, issue #54). `RELATED_WORK.md` §4 argues structurally that anti-entropy operates on exact object identity and two correct HNSW graphs over identical data differ bit-for-bit, so object-level repair cannot be pointed at the index — predicting that after chaos a replica's missing objects return while its graph quality does not. On Weaviate, at a 60 s horizon, **both axes heal**. Against a corpus-matched control the chaos arm lost no more `index_recall` than the control in any seed; on the three corpus-matched pairs the paired differences were 0.000, 0.000 and +0.015, two of them identical to within the metric's 0.005 resolution. This is null hypothesis (i) exactly as pre-registered, and the spec committed in advance to reporting it at least as prominently as a positive. **What survives is the structural claim; what fails is its predicted observable consequence** — most likely because re-inserting the objects rebuilds enough of the graph as a side effect that no deficit remains resolvable. The mechanism is not observed. **Bounded deliberately in both directions:** one system, one pinned build, one host, 60 s, and **n = 3** matched pairs after two of five seeds were right-censored, excluding a chaos-specific deficit above ~0.02 and saying nothing below it. This is not "anti-entropy repairs HNSW graphs". Recorded here because it redirects the project's central argument, which this log requires be logged rather than left in a PR comment. Four amendments and three discarded sweeps preceded the result and are worth the space: the graph axis first scored **0.23 on a healthy replica** (an L2/cosine metric mismatch plus a 14,200-object corpus leak into a 5,000-object ground truth); `docker stop` isolation dropped Raft below quorum and bricked the cluster twice; batch-deleting the corpus between runs stalled async repair permanently under `deletionStrategy: NoAutomatedResolution`; and the **first apparently-positive result was dilution** — ground truth covering 5,000 objects while the replica answered from 10,000 — caught in review by a parameter-free model that predicted all five observed values to a mean absolute error of 0.027. The pre-registered primary metric was itself wrong once both arms reached 10,000 objects, since corpus size alone satisfies "outside its own pre-chaos range"; scored as written it reported 3 of 5 dissociations that were pure corpus-size effect. See `research/weaviate_dissociation/SPEC.md`.

**Reported a pre-registered statistic and overturned it in the same section, after disaggregation reversed its answer.**
PR #59 (`research/weaviate_repair_clock/`, issue #56). The question was whether Weaviate's repair clock is anchored to the write or the restart. The pre-registered statistic — CV(`repair_s`) against CV(`age + repair`) — said **write**-anchored on every indicator: 0.766 vs 0.132, aggregate corr(age, `repair_s`) = −0.942. Splitting by regime reverses it: within young (age 2–15 s, n = 17) and old (30–38 s, n = 12) alike, `repair_s` is flat across 13 s and 8 s of age variation while `age + repair` tracks age at +0.99 — the **restart**-anchored signature. The aggregate slope comes entirely from a step between regimes (~32 s → ~2 s). **Simpson's paradox, in an experiment built to settle a question about origins.** The answer is neither: **divergence age selects the regime, and within a regime the clock runs from the restart**; the threshold is bracketed to (15 s, 30 s) and was never sampled. The transferable finding is methodological: the spec registered an *estimand* rather than a threshold — exactly what `SPEC_TEMPLATE.md` requires after #48 step 2c — and **that was still not enough**, because registering the right kind of statistic does not protect against applying it to a mixture of two populations. The statistic was checked for mechanical bias *before* being overturned (a simulated pure restart-anchored system returns RESTART at every constant tried), so it was applied to the wrong population rather than miscalibrated. Review round 1 added the part that makes the claim controlled rather than asserted: step 1b fixes the outage at 40 s, making age collinear with where in the outage the write landed, and the **absence = 10 s cell** — dropped from the write-up as an uninformative consistency check — is the only observation that separates them. It puts the write 4 s into the outage at a young age and repairs *slowly*, refuting the write-position reading. The relationship is non-monotonic in write position and monotone in age. See `research/weaviate_repair_clock/SPEC.md`.

**Scored a detector that had been recording its own answers, unexamined, through the entire Qdrant programme — and found it at chance on the axis that matters most.**
PR #57 (`research/loo_agreement_qdrant/`, issue #52). `loo_agreement` and `shard_agreement` are written into every `samples.csv` this project produces, including all the Qdrant studies: 51 runs, 3,594 populated rows, none zero. Nobody had ever scored them, because nothing in the pipeline's role selection looks at *data* — queries 0–5 all look at issues and PRs, so committed-but-unexamined evidence is invisible to the loop no matter how long it runs. That gap is now query 3b. **Zero compute** produced two results. **(1) Layer 3 replicates on a second system**, for end-to-end quality: chaos 0.908 against a 0.635 no-chaos control, p = 0.0023 by seeded permutation. **(2) On graph quality it is at chance**: 0.348 against a chance line of 0.333, versus a 0.670 baseline, separating at p = 0.0001 *in the wrong direction*. That second result is a limit on this project's own headline — the established Qdrant finding is replica-level `index_recall` divergence, and the ground-truth-free detector cannot find the replica that finding is about. Offered as hypothesis, not conclusion: `loo_agreement` compares *returned results*, so a replica missing objects is easy to flag while a replica holding all the data with a slightly worse graph returns nearly the same answers — the detector is subject to the same silence the project exists to study. Three things are recorded deliberately. **(a) The metric was not chosen after seeing the data**: `_detection_stats()` and `resolution_eps()` are imported unmodified from `replica_recall/analyze.py`, written for nano-db before any Qdrant run existed, and the SPEC was committed before any Qdrant detection number was computed. That is what makes a post-hoc analysis admissible. **(b) The no-chaos baseline is load-bearing and was pre-registered as such**: at 0.635 against a 0.333 chance line, a healthy cluster scores well above chance with nothing to detect, purely from near-ties surviving the exclusion rule — quoting the chaos figure against *chance* rather than *baseline* would have overstated the detector badly. **(c) Review caught a grouping bug that changed the conclusion**: `condition_of()` classified by substring, and `"chaos" in "nochaos"` is True, so five `..._nochaos_...` runs were scored as chaos runs. Reading the condition from each run's own `run_meta.json` instead moved the `index_recall` result from a weak null (0.469 vs 0.648, p = 0.0594) to an unambiguous collapse to chance (0.348 vs 0.670, p = 0.0001). The fix made both halves stronger. Also deviates from project practice for a stated reason: the groups are 9–20 runs, so C(25,12) = 5.2M makes exact enumeration infeasible and a seeded permutation test (200k resamples) is used, labelled per row in the output. See `research/loo_agreement_qdrant/SPEC.md`.

**Withdrew "Weaviate's repair is two discrete paths" one day after publishing it, and voided the pre-registered statistic that was meant to test it.**
PR #51 (`research/weaviate_repair_window/`, issue #48, step 2c). #49 reported repair latency as bimodal with "nothing between 0.2 s and 36 s across 18 runs" and read that as two discrete code paths. Step 2c fixed the timing offsets instead of randomizing the pre-restart delay and put **12 of 18 observations inside that interval**; the gap was an artifact of how a uniform random delay samples the latency surface, not a property of Weaviate. Two process points are recorded rather than smoothed. **(1) The pre-registration encoded the assumption it was meant to test.** The step 2c metric fixed a binary fast/slow split at 1 s *because* the gap was believed real; with the gap gone that threshold cuts condition B's tight 0.735–2.245 s cluster in half, so the "slow 4/6" and Fisher p = 0.4545 the script prints are artifacts of a dead premise. Pre-registration stopped motivated reading of the result and did not stop a bad analysis choice from being locked in — the defence is to pre-register the *estimand* and keep the dichotomization out of it. **(2) The nominated comparison was the wrong one.** A-vs-C was named discriminating; B-vs-C is the clean contrast, because it holds absence fixed and varies only divergence age. What replaces the withdrawn claim is stronger: at a fixed 40 s outage, a divergence ~38 s old reconciles in ~1 s while one ~6 s old takes ~31 s — disjoint, exact two-sided Mann-Whitney U = 0 at 6v6, p = 0.0022 — and an earlier draft called this "an older divergence repairs faster" -- **withdrawn in review round 1**, because measuring from the write rather than the restart puts B and C at 38.9 s and 36.9 s, turning a 30x gap into ~5%. Convergence at a fixed latency after the write fits equally well and would be near-definitional. The measurement stands without a mechanism; whether the repair clock is anchored to the write or the restart is the open question, and A's slow runs at ~56 s since-write rule out a single global constant. Condition A (6 s outage) is bimodal at ~0.02 s or ~52 s and is not explained by either manipulated variable; left open, no mechanism named. Also re-argued the "step, not a decay curve" conclusion from trajectories alone, since it had inherited from the withdrawn reading: it holds at 50–500 objects (transition in 8–167 ms) but not at 5,000 (5.8–6.3 s, ~5–6 points at 1 s cadence), so divergence size decides whether the dissociation experiment measures a step or a curve. See `research/weaviate_repair_window/SPEC.md` step 2c.

**Cancelled the sub-second sampling work that three merged PRs had named as the last prerequisite for the Weaviate experiment.**
PR #49 (`research/weaviate_repair_window/`, issue #48). #43 measured one async repair at ~0.3 s and #44/#45/#46 turned that single observation into a design constraint — "any Weaviate healing measurement must beat 0.3 s" — which made sub-second sampling the only thing standing between the instrument chain and the dissociation experiment. Characterizing the apparatus first, as `SPEC_TEMPLATE.md` now requires, showed the figure was one draw from a wide timing-determined distribution: the same 50-object divergence gives 44.7 s, 0.008 s, 0.010 s, and repair is independent of divergence size across 50→5,000. Sampling only ever had to beat the slow route, so **1–5 s cadence over a ≥60 s observation is sufficient and the sub-second work was never needed**. Logged here because it is a decision that stops a line of work, which this log requires be logged rather than left in a PR comment. The instrument-characterization requirement paid for itself on its first use: three prior sweeps (#11/#16, #20, #25) were voided by apparatus properties computable in advance, and this is the first time the check ran before the compute rather than after. A hard probe bound was also found by hitting it: `ids` is base64 in the URL, so a probe works to 15,000 ids and fails silently at 20,000. See `research/weaviate_repair_window/SPEC.md`.

**Made the Weaviate per-replica probe trustworthy before trusting it, and fixed a silent-topology hazard that would have invalidated every Weaviate run.**
PR #47 (`research/weaviate_probe_per_id/`, issue #46). #43's probe answered "how many bytes came back", which cannot support `completeness`, a metric defined over specific ids. Object ids turn out to be 16 bytes big-endian in the internal payload, so presence became a set rather than a count, validated against a *constructed* expectation — present, never-written, and peer-only ids in one request — because the failure mode that matters is a decoder that always succeeds. 8/8 checks, 0 false positives on never-written ids, and a diverged replica showing 0/10 peer-only ids while holding 20/20 others with peers up. Two things are recorded deliberately. **(1)** The first implementation asserted a superset rather than the pre-registered set equality; the review caught it, and the fix asserts equality at both moments where the expectation is actually defined (before and after peer-only convergence), since it is undefined across that boundary. **(2)** `create_class` tolerated HTTP 422 and silently ran against a stale auto-schema class that was **3 shards at replication factor 1** — sharded, not replicated — which would have produced per-replica numbers from a topology that had no replicas. `verify_class()` now checks the topology it got rather than the one it asked for. The image is pinned by digest for the same reason: the probe depends on an undocumented internal API of one specific build. See `research/weaviate_probe_per_id/SPEC.md`.

**Accepted an undocumented internal API as the Weaviate probe, making the Weaviate experiment asymmetric by design.**
PR #45 (`research/weaviate_nonperturbing_probe/`, issue #43). #42's isolation probe worked but left the probed node UNHEALTHY for ten minutes, so per-sample probing was impossible and the Weaviate leg was bounded to a snapshot. A cluster-internal API on `CLUSTER_DATA_BIND_PORT` (7947) returns one replica's own objects with peers up — 20/20 probes at 5 s cadence against the isolation probe's 1 — and it is provably local: it refuses when the node is down, and returned 0 bytes while peers returned 31,190 on the same request. `_search` on that port returns 415 across 12 content types, so **`index_recall` stays snapshot-only while `completeness` can be sampled continuously.** That asymmetry is accepted rather than engineered around, and the eventual Weaviate claim has to carry it: the data axis is a time series, the graph axis is two endpoints. The dependency is deliberate and stated — an undocumented API is not a contract, which is why #46 pins the image by digest. See `research/weaviate_nonperturbing_probe/SPEC.md`.

**Fixed a harness defect that had already cost one pre-registered seed by producing a normal-looking run with no data in it.**

**Gave the pipeline's role selection a query for `stage:changes-requested` PRs, and capped the review/revision cycle at three rounds rather than letting it run unbounded.**
The original query list had no condition matching a PR the reviewer had sent back: query 1 looked for `stage:in-review` PRs and query 2 for unassigned `stage:proposed` issues, while a revised PR's linked issue is both assigned and out of `stage:proposed`. Requested revisions were therefore invisible to every tick, and the loop would have moved on to filing new research questions with work in flight unaddressed — the inverse of the downstream-first priority the section claims to implement. **Ruled out pure concurrency and the duplicate-insert bug as the mechanism behind the 58.7%-loss anomalous replica, rather than accepting either on inference.**
Both were plausible from reading the code. Both were tested with deterministic, isolated reproductions (16 concurrent writers / zero chaos for the first; a scripted duplicate-insert-then-bulk-insert for the second) rather than accepted because they were consistent with the observation. Both came back clean. The duplicate-insert bug was real anyway (silently overwrote a vector's data on re-insert) and was fixed regardless of not explaining the anomaly — a bug doesn't need to explain your headline result to be worth fixing. Root cause of the anomaly is still open. See `postmortems/catastrophic-disconnection.md`.

**Fixed the duplicate-insert bug as "no-op if the incoming data is identical, reject if it differs" rather than always rejecting.**
`RemoveShard`'s rebalance path is documented as idempotent and can legitimately re-migrate a key that already landed on a shard — an unconditional reject would have broken that. See `cluster/shard_service_impl.hpp` and the fix commit.

**Changed `node_locks_` from a growing `std::vector` to a fixed 4,096-entry striped pool.**
The growing-vector design had the lock array's growth nested inside the storage-resize branch, so it silently stopped growing once storage pre-allocation covered enough nodes (the original recall-bug root cause). The fix wasn't "grow it correctly" — a fixed striped pool removes a second latent bug (reallocating a vector while other threads index into it is a race) at the same time. See `postmortems/recall-bugs.md`, Bug 2.

**Chose real SIFT1M over uniform-random synthetic vectors as the corpus for the replica-recall experiment.**
Uniform-random 128-d vectors suffer distance concentration — the true top-k becomes close to arbitrary, and recall falls with N for reasons unrelated to replication. Measured directly: `index_recall` showed no separation between baseline and chaos on uniform data (p = 0.31) but separated cleanly on SIFT1M (p = 0.0079). This was tested, not assumed — a benchmark built on the wrong corpus would have concluded there was nothing to find. See `research/replica_recall/README.md`, "Choosing a corpus."

**Chose the exact two-sided Mann-Whitney U test over a t-test for baseline-vs-chaos comparison.**
Five runs per condition is too few to lean on a normality assumption, and the metrics (recall, completeness) are bounded proportions, not naturally normal. Rank-based avoids that assumption. Tradeoff accepted and documented: at n=5 vs n=5 the smallest attainable p-value is 2/252 ≈ 0.0079, so a "significant" result at that floor indicates the groups separate completely, not that the effect is necessarily large — judge magnitude from the means, significance from the p. See `research/replica_recall/README.md`, "The seed sweep."

**Probes bypass the coordinator and hit each replica's gRPC port directly.**
Going through the coordinator would merge replica responses via scatter-gather, which is exactly the mechanism that would hide the divergence under study. This is a deliberate deviation from how a real client would query the cluster, made because the research question is about per-replica state, not client-observed behavior.

**Score `loo_agreement` per-replica (leave-one-out) rather than using aggregate shard-level agreement as the detection signal.**
Aggregate cross-replica agreement is close to tautological: when every replica misses the same hard queries, agreement collapses onto recall whether or not anything is broken, and it reads ~1.0 on a healthy cluster too — it doesn't discriminate. Per-replica peer agreement asks the sharper, operationally relevant question: which replica should I distrust. See `research/replica_recall/README.md`, "What is measured."

**Judge the healing/quiesce result on absolute missing-write count, not on the `completeness` ratio.**
`completeness` is a ratio, and writes keep flowing during the post-chaos observation window — a growing denominator drags the ratio toward 1.0 even when zero missed writes have actually returned (documented concretely as "the dilution trap": completeness climbed 0.9607 → 0.9694 while the absolute missing count stayed flat at 592 across three post-chaos windows). Using the ratio would have reported false recovery. See commit `research: judge healing on absolute missing writes, not the ratio` and `research/replica_recall/README.md`, "The dilution trap."

**Never read graph-forensics data off a live cluster; always tear it down first (`forensics_experiment.py`).**
`element_count` in the on-disk header lags the actual node writes by design (the node body is persisted before the counter is bumped), so a mid-write snapshot of a live cluster looks exactly like corruption. The first forensics attempt measured a running cluster and reported 168 dangling edges that turned out to be nothing. See `postmortems/catastrophic-disconnection.md`.
